# Sophie's perspectives playbook: A seminar

**The question it answers:** perspectives, "On an open question, what are the views, and what does each rest on?"
([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md)). Afterwards the viewer can state the strongest
version of each position and its cost, and knows where they stand and why.

**How it meets the must-haves:**

- **Each position in its strongest version:** written at its strongest before it enters, the narration saying "at its
  strongest, it goes like this"; after being caught out, it gets one reply.
- **The same case tests all of them:** one set of cases per episode, one change at a time; every view answers on this
  set, and the answers are recorded on the position board.
- **No verdict for the viewer:** the last seat is left for the viewer. When the presenter has a position, say it; if
  not, say so.

**Use it for:** questions on which reasonable people still disagree after the facts are checked and the reasoning is
done. The disagreement comes from what is weighed most: consequences or rights, compatriots or all humanity, liberty or
equality. For example:

- "Are there things you must not do, whatever the consequences?"
- "Should rich countries open their borders?"
- "Should the rich be taxed more to support the poor?"
- "Do people have free will?"

**Versus other presenters:** at present only Sophie has a playbook for perspectives. Theo's, Fabian's and Mason's ways
all head toward one result: an inevitable answer, an outcome that grows, a fact that runs out. Perspectives must not
converge.

**Not this one:**

- A claim whose evidence could in principle settle it ("Does central bank independence lower inflation?") is argument:
  data and real outcomes go to Theo or Fabian; cases and original texts go to Sophie's "Try a claim"
  ([argument.md](argument.md)).
- Already settled, telling how it came about, is conceptual change.

How to arrange a set of cases, the beat of a verdict, every view at its strongest, the big picture and the accounts,
honesty and language are in her script craft ([craft.md](../craft.md)); the picture is in the style
([style.md](../style.md)).

## Skeleton

Two arrangements; choose by the sources:

- When the sources pose one question with a few cases, **sections by case** (Sandel).
- When the sources present several thinkers, each with their own account, **sections by position** (Big Questions).

Both must have the same set of cases, with one change at a time.

### Sections by case

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8% | The first case | The main question in one line, unchanged for the whole episode; one sketch, told within 40 seconds; the verdict card, a 3-second pause |
| 8–20% | Two intuitions | The position board appears with two columns, one plain-language claim each; one sentence for each side, the minority side also in its strongest wording |
| 20–50% | One change at a time: two or three variations | Each variation is one beat ([craft.md](../craft.md#b-the-beat-of-a-verdict)): say what alone changed and which column it tests, judge, update the account; the cell where what the principle says does not match "if you chose A last time" lights up |
| 50–62% | Step back: attach the names | The column titles turn over: the plain-language claim plus the school and the person's name, with a passage card each; say "there are this many kinds" |
| 62–85% | A real case | A lawsuit or a work; once the facts are told, the viewer judges first; then one or two variations with one change each; finally the real verdict is revealed, only as one side's voice |
| 85–100% | Close | The whole position board zooms out: each column's answers and costs on this set of cases; where the presenter stands (or that they do not); where you stand is up to you; answer the opening question |

### Sections by position

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–12% | Opening: two intuitions fight | A concrete case for you to judge; change one thing and judge again; say aloud the two principles you hold that fight each other. The main-question line is there from second 0 |
| 12–15% | The line-up | One name card per view, the plain-language claim large, the person's name small; say which set of cases will test them |
| 15–75% | One section per view (at most three) | The name card enters → its answers on this set of cases, marked in its colour on the case's picture → one key idea, one example → hit by a case with one change (the viewer judges first) → it replies once → the position board records a row. At each seam say two things: what has just been established, and which hole the next view will fill |
| 75–90% | Zoom out | The whole position board: each column's answer and cost on every case; one comparison: whichever two choices you made at the opening, that is the column you stand in |
| 90–100% | Close | Where the presenter stands (or that they do not); where you stand is up to you; no swap to a new question |

## Short version (6 minutes or less)

- One case, two variations, two columns.
- Either attaching the names or the real case, not both.
- Zooming out on the position board at the close can never be cut.

## Quality bar

After watching, the viewer can say:

- each column's strongest sentence;
- on which case it paid a price;
- on which case they themselves turned, and why.

Remembering only "what the various schools say", being unable to say any school's cost, or feeling that the presenter
actually chose for them, does not count.

## Self-check (in addition to the craft self-check)

- [ ] Every view answers on the same set of cases, with answers recorded on the position board; no section is "now
      another person gives their own account".
- [ ] Every column was tested by at least one case, and replied once after being caught out.
- [ ] The close did not judge for the viewer; the presenter's position was said (or that there is none).
- [ ] The main question never changed from start to finish, and the close answered it.

# Sophie's concept playbook: Draw a line

Contents: [Skeleton](#skeleton) · [When the goal is use](#when-the-goal-is-use) ·
[Short version](#short-version-6-minutes-or-less) · [Quality bar](#quality-bar) ·
[Self-check](#self-check-in-addition-to-the-craft-self-check)

**The question it answers:** concept, "What is it, and how does it differ from things that look like it?"
([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md)). Afterwards the viewer can tell examples from
non-examples and produce one of their own. Here the concept is a moral or legal line.

**How it meets the must-haves:**

- **At least two examples:** the opening case is the first. The second comes from it by changing one thing: different on
  the surface, on the same side of the line. Both are rows that every version of the definition judges "yes", and the
  viewer judges "yes" too.
- **A close non-example:** a case that fits the viewer's first definition, yet the viewer themselves judges "no"
  (saying something false without lying; getting something by the rules without deserving it). It is the first breach.
- **The definition comes out of the difference:** put side by side the pair that differ in one place and that the
  viewer judges differently; that one place is the sentence the definition was missing. Strike out the old definition,
  write the new one beside it, and go back to judge every row again.
- **A boundary case the viewer judges:** at the close, a case sitting on the line, where the repaired definition wears
  thin, or two ways of drawing the line judge differently. The verdict card, a 3-second pause, one sentence for each
  choice.
- **Produce one of their own:** the takeaway card says "from any example, change just this one thing and it becomes a
  non-example".

**Use it for:** a line drawn by human judgment, where once all the facts are known you still have to ask "does this
count?". For example:

- "How does deserving differ from being entitled?"
- "How does killing differ from letting die?"
- "What counts as consent?"
- "Where is the line between self-defence and revenge after the fact?"
- "How does a refugee differ from a migrant?"

Best when the sources themselves have rulings, statutes or a thinker's own text drawing the line: they are the real
cases, and the definition board's second column.

**Versus other presenters' concept playbooks** (Theo, Fabian, Mason and Sophie all have one; choose by the shape of the
answer, [presenters/README.md](../../README.md)):

- **Theo:** builds the definition step by step from examples and non-examples, and the definition can be reasoned out.
  When the concept is a structure (a conditional probability, a kind of mathematical object), it goes to Theo.
- **Fabian:** two small worlds that differ in only one condition, running to different outcomes; the boundary can be
  judged by applying the conditions. When the concept is about the situation of actors (an institution, a kind of
  competition), it goes to Fabian.
- **Mason:** sweeps a parameter, the examples, non-examples and boundary run out of a toy, and the definition is a
  measurable check. When the concept is a behaviour of a system, it goes to Mason.
- **Sophie:** no world will run this line out for you; the evidence is your judgment of cases. She first asks you to
  say where your line is, tests it with cases that differ in one place, and repairs it from where your judgments
  differ; sometimes what needs repairing is a judgment of yours that bias pulled off course, and she says so. When the
  line is still disputed, at most three ways of drawing it sit side by side, each with its cost.

**Not this one:**

- The viewer wants whether this line matters and whether to judge by it (is killing really worse than letting die?
  should euthanasia be legal?): that is perspectives ([perspectives.md](perspectives.md)) or argument
  ([argument.md](argument.md)). A concept episode asks only where the line is; how each side of the line should be
  treated gets one sentence, left for the next episode.
- After repair two reasonable ways of drawing the line remain, and those two are the protagonists (is consent a matter
  of the mind, or of what is said?): that is perspectives.
- The viewer wants to judge a case step by step by statute (checking each element in turn): that is procedure, which
  Sophie has no playbook for.
- How the line came to be drawn where it is today: that is conceptual change
  ([conceptual-change.md](conceptual-change.md)).
- When the concept is a structure, a situation or a behaviour of a system, choose another presenter (above).

How to arrange a set of cases, the beat of a verdict, every view at its strongest, the big picture and the accounts,
honesty and language are in her script craft ([craft.md](../craft.md)); the picture is in the style
([style.md](../style.md)). In this playbook the position board is called the **definition board**: the columns are
versions of the definition (your first version, the repaired one, another way of drawing it; at most three columns),
the rows are cases. Each cell holds two marks: how this version of the definition judges (solid), and how most people's
intuition judges (hollow, marked only with a source; without a source, leave it unmarked and have the narration say "if
you judged…"). A cell where they do not match is boxed. The video does not know what the viewer judged, so every
judgment is told as two paths with "if you judged…".

## Skeleton

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8% | A case: you judge first | The main question in one line, unchanged for the whole episode ("What counts as X? How does it differ from Y?"); one sketch, told within 40 seconds; the verdict card "counts / does not count", a 3-second pause |
| 8–15% | Your definition | "Which sentence were you relying on just now?" The narration says the most common one (the sources' or everyday wording), hung as the definition board's first column, labelled "intuition"; it works on the opening case, so let the viewer stand on it first |
| 15–35% | Two breaches, one too wide and one too narrow | Each from the same sketch with one change: one fits the definition yet you judge "does not count"; one does not fit yet you judge "counts". The viewer judges first, then sees how the first column judges, and the mismatched cell is boxed; one sentence for each choice. One of them may be a real case |
| 35–50% | Repair: the pair judged differently | Put side by side a pair that differ in one place and that most people would judge differently ("if you judged these two differently…"); that one place is the missing sentence. Strike out the first column's title and write the second version beside it; go back and judge every row again, and it counts as repaired only when all match. Only now attach names: how the statute, the ruling or the thinker draws it, with a short passage read |
| 50–65% | Another way of drawing it (when the line is disputed) | The second way enters in its strongest version; a case on which the two columns judge differently, the viewer judging first; what each column pays: whom it wrongly counts in, whom it wrongly leaves out; the column caught out replies once. When the line is not disputed, this section becomes a second repair |
| 65–80% | A real case | A lawsuit or a regulation: once the facts are told, the viewer judges first with the repaired definition; then reveal how the court or the statute draws it. How the law draws it now is a fact; whether it should be drawn that way is not; when either differs from the viewer's judgment, say so |
| 80–100% | The boundary is yours; take it away | A case sitting on the line, the verdict card, a 3-second pause, one sentence for each path; the whole definition board zooms out; a three-line takeaway card: what the line is (the sentence everyone agrees on, plus the part still argued over), why the most alike non-example does not count, and which one thing to change to turn any example into a non-example |

Measured pace: the Big Questions video on consent spends 5 minutes 43 seconds on three breaches and two repairs, about 1
minute 50 seconds per breach; Luo Xiang's "provocation" passage goes through about ten one-change cases in 6 minutes,
30 to 40 seconds each, but never pauses once; Sandel's three contrasting pairs take about 9 minutes, 3 minutes a pair.
Sophie gives one beat per case ([craft.md](../craft.md#b-the-beat-of-a-verdict)): the sketch within 40 seconds, a
3-second pause after the verdict, one sentence of account, 1 to 1.5 minutes per beat.

## When the goal is use

No playbook ([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md#concept-the-other-goal-use)): using a value
line to judge new cases still asks "does this count?", so follow the skeleton above; using a principle to judge "should
it be done?" is argument or perspectives.

## Short version (6 minutes or less)

- One case, your definition, one breach (the near miss), one repair from a pair differing in one place, one boundary.
- Either the second breach or the real case, not both.
- The boundary beat and the "change just this one thing" takeaway card can never be cut.

## Quality bar

After watching, the viewer:

- given a case not in the video, can judge which side of the line it falls on and say which one place decides it;
- can say why the most alike non-example does not count;
- can produce an example of their own, and knows which one thing to change to turn it into a non-example;
- knows which part of the line everyone agrees on and which part is still argued over.

Remembering only the terms, thinking the definition is just the presenter's opinion, or sorting the cases in the video
but failing to judge a new one, does not count.

## Self-check (in addition to the craft self-check)

- [ ] Before the first definition appeared, the viewer judged the opening case; the first version is what the viewer
      would say themselves, labelled "intuition".
- [ ] Both breaches come from the same sketch with one change, one too wide and one too narrow; when there is only one
      direction, why was said.
- [ ] The new definition was drawn from a pair that differ in one place and were judged differently; after repair every
      row was judged again, and all match.
- [ ] When what needs changing is a judgment and not the definition (a judgment pulled off course by bias), this was
      said, along with on what grounds.
- [ ] When the line is disputed, the other way of drawing it entered in its strongest version, and each column said
      whom it wrongly counts in and whom it wrongly leaves out; nothing was chosen for the viewer.
- [ ] For a line the law or the sources have already drawn, who drew it and where was made clear; where it differs
      from the viewer's judgment, that was said.
- [ ] For the closing boundary case, the viewer judged first, with a 3-second pause and one sentence for each choice;
      the takeaway card says "change just this one thing, and the example becomes a non-example".
- [ ] Cases of sex, violence or death have no jokes and use no details of real victims.

# Theo's mechanism playbook: Explain a mechanism

**The question it answers:** mechanism, "Why does this happen? How does it work?"
([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md)). Afterwards the viewer can tell the causal chain in
their own words and predict what changes when one condition changes.

**How it meets the must-haves:** every link of the causal chain is built on screen step by step, with no link skipped
(rebuild, do not relay). A guess beat lets the viewer first predict what changes when one condition changes, then
reveals it.

**Use it for:** for example "Why does a prism split light?", "What really are the real numbers?", "What does the
attention mechanism do?". One episode turns around one main question, one aha and one punchline.

**Not this one:** the viewer wants how the parts work together (system: [system.md](system.md)), why a claim deserves
belief (argument: [argument.md](argument.md)), or how an idea came about (conceptual change:
[conceptual-change.md](conceptual-change.md)). When the viewer should do it themselves, it is procedure
([procedure.md](procedure.md), or Mason's playbook; [presenters/README.md](../../README.md)). The course of events, or
several positions side by side: Theo has no playbook for those ([presenter.md](../presenter.md)); choose another
presenter.

**The core idea of this playbook is an aha.** One aha moment carries one video (principle 6 in
[teaching/rules.md](../../../teaching/rules.md#principles-every-presenter-follows)). If you cannot find the moment where
the view shifts and the pieces fall into place by themselves, do not start. Mathematics and physics topics also need:
**rebuild, do not relay** (principle 9). Do not be a second-hand commentator; find the path that makes the result "look
almost inevitable" (what Grant calls an unsolved expository problem). What "done" means in other subjects is in
principle 9.

**There are two kinds of foil**, chosen from the audience description. When the viewer has a ready-made textbook
account in their head, state it fairly and then prosecute it ([craft.md](../craft.md#i-motivation), technique 3). When
the viewer has a reasonable but wrong intuition, say that intuition and let it fail
([teaching/rules.md](../../../teaching/rules.md#handling-a-misconception)). The two can be used together.

## Skeleton

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2% | Hook | The first 5 seconds are something concrete (a picture, a question, a counter-intuitive fact), not a title card |
| 2–10% | Foil | State the "standard account" fairly |
| 10–15% | Prosecution and standard | "None of this is false, but none of it says why"; 3–4 escalating questions; say "what we want is for it to feel discovered" |
| 15–20% | Roadmap | Previewed as a "promise", with one strange preview (which must be paid off later); a statement of scope; the place in the series |
| 20–40% | Act one | The restated question, the spine object, the first key point |
| 40–45% | Recap | A causal-chain recap (not a list of topics) |
| 45–75% | Acts two and three | The model, show before deriving, read the formula term by term, extreme cases, real evidence, pay off the preview |
| 75–85% | Punchline | "So this is the real reason ___" |
| 85–95% | Coda | Interrogate your own toy model / a counter-intuitive extension / say plainly what was left out / open questions |
| 95–100% | Close | A reframe, not a summary; hand off to the next episode; thanks and sources |

Measured pace (3b1b; the numbers are in the style, [style.md](../style.md#measurements)): about 180–195 wpm in English;
a new element on screen every 7 to 9 seconds; a large rearrangement every 12 to 15 seconds; a completely new
composition every 20 to 50 seconds; fewer than 1 hard cut a minute (only around live footage).

## Short version (6 minutes or less)

The long skeleton was measured from videos of 15 to 25 minutes. Copied into a short video, the "standard account"
alone eats half the time. When the plan's length is 6 minutes or less, use this skeleton instead. Use it too when this
question type is a short passage inside another episode:

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5% | Hook | The first 5 seconds are something concrete; the main question is asked within 10 seconds |
| 5–15% | The standard account and its problem | One or two sentences of the familiar account and where it falls short (at most 30 seconds); may be left out when the viewer has no ready-made account |
| 15–75% | One or two acts | Only one aha and one spine object; concrete example first, then the name, then the generalization |
| 75–90% | Punchline | "So this is the real reason ___" |
| 90–100% | Close | One sentence of reframe; when needed, one "if you want to go deeper…" |

**Leave out:** the roadmap and the strange preview, recaps between acts, a long coda, the place in the series.
**Keep:** make the viewer want it before giving the answer, concrete before abstract, naming after understanding,
every line of narration pointing at the picture, honesty.
**Word count:** convert with the language reference's speaking rate.

## Self-check

- [ ] The main question can be said in one sentence, and has a naive version and a restated version.
- [ ] The first 5 seconds are a concrete hook, not a title card.
- [ ] (Long version) the strange preview in the roadmap is paid off later; for the short version write "not
      applicable to the short version".
- [ ] The close is a reframe, not a summary.

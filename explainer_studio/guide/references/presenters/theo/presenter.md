# Theo

**What kind of teacher:** the teacher at the blackboard.
**How he makes people understand:** he rebuilds the idea step by step in pictures, until the result looks almost
inevitable.
**What the viewer does:** watches the idea being built, block by block; where they want to know, they think for a
moment first, then see it confirmed.
**What the picture is:** the idea itself: a number line, vectors, a machine, a table.
**Real examples:** they are the protagonist. Dig into one, let the whole episode turn around it, then apply it quickly
elsewhere.
**Naming:** understand first; the name comes after.
**Most common mistake:** following the sources' own cases, so one episode switches between several times and places,
and stacking metaphors onto the sentences that carry the reasoning.
**Name:** Theo, which brings to mind theory and theorem.

Everything above is the presenter himself: it holds for every question type he has a playbook for. What holds for
only one question type is written in that playbook ([presenters/README.md](../README.md)).

| From | What |

## Playbooks

What each question type is, what the viewer can do afterwards and its must-haves are in
[teaching/question-types.md](../../teaching/question-types.md).

| Question type | Playbook | Why it fits him |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | [Explain a mechanism](question-types/mechanism.md) | 3Blue1Brown's home ground: rebuild a "why" until it is almost inevitable |
| Argument | [Test an argument](question-types/argument.md) | Empirical and technical claims: first build the core idea on one example, then read the evidence |
| Conceptual change | [How an idea changed](question-types/conceptual-change.md) | Rebuild the old idea so it is reasonable, then let it fail |
| System | [Walk through a system](question-types/system.md) | First a faint division-of-labour diagram, then grow the system block by block from a version that almost works, each block patching one hole in the previous version; follow the same thing through every part, and finally try removing a block |
| Proof | [Find the proof](question-types/proof.md) | Rebuilding until the result is "almost inevitable" is exactly what a proof asks for |
| Concept | [Enclose a concept](question-types/concept.md) | Building a definition step by step from examples and counterexamples is what he does all the time; for the goal of use, see that section of the playbook |
| Procedure | [Reinvent a procedure](question-types/procedure.md) | Steps that can be rebuilt into a procedure that "could not be otherwise" (a calculation, an algorithm worked by hand, how to write a proof): he states each step's judgment as inevitable, and in a new situation lets the viewer guess the next step first. A skill that needs watching a real person's hands (operating equipment, body movement) is not his way |

**Not a fit** (his way conflicts with the question type's must-haves; choose another presenter):

- **Perspectives:** his way heads toward one inevitable answer; perspectives must not give the viewer a verdict.
- **Narrative:** he rebuilds ideas; a narrative needs people and turning points to carry the main line.

Every playbook shares his script craft ([craft.md](craft.md): 38 techniques, the principles Grant has stated, the
writing procedure, the self-check) and his style ([style.md](style.md): the blackboard).

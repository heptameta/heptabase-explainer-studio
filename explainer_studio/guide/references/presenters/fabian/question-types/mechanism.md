# Fabian's mechanism playbook: Explain a mechanism

**The question it answers:** mechanism, "Why does this happen? How does it work?"
([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md)). Afterwards the viewer can tell the causal chain in
their own words and predict what changes when one condition changes.

**How it meets the must-haves:** every link of the causal chain is a step an actor in the small world takes by the
rules: one more stroke on the spine picture, one more conclusion on the board, visible, with no link skipped. "Predict
what changes when one condition changes" is a new round: change only one condition, let the viewer guess first, then
run it for them.

**Use it for:** mechanisms whose answer grows out of many actors each acting on their own interests or rules and
affecting each other. The actors can be people, companies or countries, or individuals treated as having interests
(competing genes, a spreading pathogen). For example "Why don't those in power make the country rich first?", "Why
does this electoral system end up with only two parties?", "Why did disease travel in only one direction?", "Why does
everyone know they should cooperate, and still each grab for themselves?". Best when the sources are full of countries,
eras and people but the real question is one causal chain of incentives.

**Versus Theo's "Explain a mechanism":** Theo rebuilds a structure; the picture is the idea itself, and the viewer
watches it and feels "of course it has to be this way". When the answer is still there with every actor removed
(physics, mathematics, programs, molecular machines), it goes to Theo or Mason (see the fourth question in
[presenters/README.md](../../README.md#the-same-question-type-several-presenters-playbooks)). Fabian sits the viewer in
a seat and plays a round, and the answer grows out of everyone's choices; when the sources are full of countries, eras
and people but the real question is one causal chain of incentives, he gathers them into one world.

**Not this one:** the viewer wants how events happened, who did what and when (narrative), or several views side by side
(perspectives): choose another presenter. Proving that something must hold (proof), or having the viewer do it
themselves (procedure): Fabian has no playbook for those ([presenter.md](../presenter.md)).

How to build the small world, one step of running the rules, putting the viewer in a seat, objections and failures,
and language are in his script craft ([craft.md](../craft.md)); the picture is in the style ([style.md](../style.md)).

## Skeleton

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–7% | Strange phenomenon and puzzle | A concrete, anomalous fact; the main question in one sentence (ideally: why isn't it the other way round?); say the viewer's intuition, and doubt it |
| 7–15% | The shrunken world | The actors and the premise; say whose model it is and that it is simplified; words that need telling apart get one small card with two conditions |
| 15–60% | Running the rules, two to four steps | Each step as in [craft.md](../craft.md#b-one-step-of-running-the-rules): one why, one rule, one actor moving once, one conclusion on the board, a pause. Between steps, voice the most obvious rebuttal for the viewer and answer it; at the hardest step the viewer takes that seat |
| 60–72% | A string of strange phenomena | With the small world's rules, untangle 2 to 4 real strange phenomena in one sentence and one picture each; real things get one cell each |
| 72–82% | Change one condition and run again | A guess: change only one condition: what happens? Stop, then run |
| 82–93% | Where the model fails | At least one real counterexample, acted out with the same icons, showing where the model is wrong; keep apart what the author says, what most scholars agree on, and what is still argued over |
| 93–100% | Close | Map the small world's words back onto the sources' terms; hand the question back to the viewer or to the next episode; no summary |

## Short version (6 minutes or less)

Only two steps of running the rules; only one or two strange phenomena; "where the model fails" shrinks to one card,
but can never be cut.

## Quality bar

After watching, the viewer can say: the small world's premise, who did what at each step, what happens when one
condition changes, and where this model is wrong. Remembering only the story, being unable to say the book's terms, or
feeling that this is "inevitably so" does not count.

## Self-check (in addition to the craft self-check)

- [ ] Every link of the causal chain is a step in the small world; no step is merely announced by the narration.
- [ ] At least one new round, changing only one condition, with a pause before running to let the viewer think (ask
      only when they would really want to; [teaching/rules.md](../../../teaching/rules.md#guess-beats)); the viewer can
      say which condition changed.
- [ ] The string of strange phenomena is untangled with one and the same set of rules; no rule was added for one
      phenomenon.

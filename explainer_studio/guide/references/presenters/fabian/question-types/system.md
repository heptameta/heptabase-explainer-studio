# Fabian's system playbook: Walk through a system

**The question it answers:** system, "How do the parts of this system work together?"
([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md)). Afterwards the viewer can, given a part, say what
it is responsible for and whom a change to it would affect.

**How it meets the must-haves:**

- **The whole picture first:** the system is the map of a small world: one icon per actor (part), with one sentence
  beside it saying what the actor wants and what the rules let them do.
- **Follow one concrete thing through every part:** a ballot, a tax payment, a court case, from start to finish,
  stopping one beat at each actor: what they did, and why they did it that way (their incentive).
- **Who a change to one part affects:** change one rule, first ask how each actor would change what they do (including
  exploiting loopholes), then walk it through again and see whether the outcome changed. Components do not change their
  behaviour because a rule changed; people do.

**Use it for:** systems whose parts are actors who make choices: an electoral system, how a law gets passed, how a
court tries a case, how an organization splits its budget, how an alliance makes its members keep their commitments.
When the answer is a causal chain (how raising interest rates brings prices down), it is mechanism, not system.

**Versus Theo's "Walk through a system":** Theo grows the system block by block from a version that almost works, each
block patching a hole, following the same thing (a request, a transaction) through every part; it fits systems whose
components behave as designed, like programs, protocols and data flows. Fabian's system is made of actors with their own
interests, and each station asks "what does this actor want, and what do the rules let them do"; when a rule changes,
the viewer takes an actor's seat and thinks how they would change what they do, then sees what the system produces once
everyone changes together, which is often not what the rule's designers wanted.

**Not this one:** when the answer is a causal chain (why does this happen), it is mechanism
([mechanism.md](mechanism.md)); when the viewer wants to learn to operate the system themselves (how to file taxes, how
to vote), it is procedure, which Fabian has no playbook for.

How to build the small world, one step of running the rules, and putting the viewer in a seat are in his script craft
([craft.md](../craft.md)); the picture is in the style ([style.md](../style.md)).

## Skeleton

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8% | A strange outcome | This system once produced a surprising outcome (the candidate with fewer votes won; a law passed that nobody wanted); the main question in one sentence |
| 8–20% | The whole picture | The system's small world: the actors, what each wants, the rules; one map that every later beat returns to |
| 20–55% | Follow one thing through | One beat per station: what this actor did, and why; the conclusion on the board, a pause. At the most important station the viewer takes that seat ("You're on this committee: would you let it through?") |
| 55–70% | Back to the strange outcome | Use the walk just finished to untangle the opening's strange outcome |
| 70–85% | Change one rule | A guess: with this rule changed, sitting in some actor's seat, how would you change what you do? Walk it through again, marking on the map the actors who changed and the outcome nobody expected |
| 85–93% | The small world against the real one | What the real system has in addition, and where the small world is wrong |
| 93–100% | Close | Ask the viewer about a part the video never reached: what is it responsible for, whom would a change to it affect; map the small world's words back onto the real system's names |

## Short version (6 minutes or less)

The whole picture in one diagram; the walk-through stopping only at the three or four key stations; change one rule
and walk it again; one sentence on how the real one differs.

## Quality bar

Given a part, the viewer can say what it is responsible for, what its people want, and who changes along with it when
it changes. Remembering only the order of the flow, without being able to say why each actor acts as they do, does not
count.

## Self-check (in addition to the craft self-check)

- [ ] The whole picture came at the start, with what each actor wants and can do written beside it.
- [ ] The same thing was followed from start to finish, and at each station why this actor acts as they do was said.
- [ ] One rule was changed and walked through again, with a pause before running, and the actors who changed were
      marked.
- [ ] The close tests the viewer with a part the video never reached.

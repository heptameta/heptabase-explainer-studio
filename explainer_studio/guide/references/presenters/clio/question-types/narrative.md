# Clio's narrative playbook: Walk the crossroads

**The question it answers:** narrative, "What happened, and why did it happen that way?"
([teaching/question-types.md](../../../teaching/question-types.md)). Afterwards the viewer can tell the main line, the
turning points and their causes, and where things could have gone otherwise.

**How it meets the must-haves:**

- **One main-line question:** one main question stated in the first minute and answered in one paragraph at the close;
  other questions become sections.
- **Every turning point has its cause:** each turning point is a crossroads, with the sides' goal cards side by side,
  saying why someone took this road using what could be seen at the time.
- **Point out where it could have gone otherwise:** the road not taken stays on the map as a dashed line, with one
  sentence, "if at the time…"; at the close the dashed lines light up again, one beat each, and add up to "it could
  have gone otherwise".

**Use it for:** topics where how it happened that one time only makes sense with particular people, timing and chance.
For example:

- "Why did Japan attack Pearl Harbor?"
- "How did half of Mexico become the United States?"
- "Why was Luther, a mere monk, not burned like Hus?"
- "Why did TSMC win?"
- "Why did America's first constitution fail?"

**Versus other presenters:** at present only Clio has a playbook for narrative. Theo makes the result look inevitable;
Fabian's small world removes names and chance, so the answer holds elsewhere too. Clio wants exactly the opposite: that
one time happened only once, and it nearly went otherwise.

**Not this one:**

- When the answer remains with names, years and chance removed ("Why don't those in power let the country get rich?"),
  it is mechanism: it goes to Fabian or Theo.
- When historians hold several views on the event, with no verdict for the viewer, it is perspectives: it goes to
  Sophie, often scheduled after this episode.
- When the protagonist is an idea, it is conceptual change: by default it goes to Theo; when the change of idea falls on
  two dated decisions, it goes to Clio's conceptual change playbook ([conceptual-change.md](conceptual-change.md)).

How to draw the big-picture map, the beat of a crossroads, zooming in and out, honesty, and names and years are in her
script craft ([craft.md](../craft.md)); the picture is in the style ([style.md](../style.md)).

## Skeleton

| Share | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6% | The ending first | A dated picture → the ending and its cost → a one-sentence main question → "to answer it, we have to go back to ___". The big-picture map appears for the first time, with the destination drawn faintly; zoom all the way out, then say which stretch this episode covers |
| 6–14% | The main line and the people | Each side's portrait goes onto the map, each person with a goal card (the side being pushed too); the main line becomes an icon that stays on the map |
| 14–75% | The walk: three to four crossroads | Each one as in [craft.md](../craft.md#b-the-beat-of-a-crossroads): the event changes one stroke on the map → goal cards side by side, two or three arrows → the viewer guesses first, a 3-second pause → why they took this road → the road not taken stays as a dashed line → "so". For the most important one, say plainly "this is where the whole road turns" |
| Details within a section | Zoom in | Zoom in on the big-picture map; a document, someone's own words or an object carries one turning point; when zooming back out, that point has been coloured. Every minute or two, the big-picture map fast-forwards from the start to now |
| 75–88% | What they reckoned on, and what happened | What the people expected vs the real outcome; where chance came in |
| 88–100% | Close | The big-picture map replays fast from the start, the whole chain is said once along the main line, and the opening's main question is answered; the dashed lines light up again, one beat each ([craft.md](../craft.md#b-the-beat-of-a-crossroads), step 5); one sentence on the consequences; one sentence on where the same thing happened again later |

## Short version (6 minutes or less)

- Keep only two crossroads; "what they reckoned on" merges into the last crossroads.
- The closing replay and the answer to the main question can never be cut.

## Quality bar

After watching, the viewer can say:

- the answer to the main question (a causal chain in one paragraph);
- at least two crossroads: which roads there were at the time, and why this one was taken;
- where it could have gone otherwise.

Remembering only the order of a string of events, or feeling that "it was always bound to happen", does not count.

## Self-check (in addition to the craft self-check)

- [ ] The first minute stated a one-sentence main question, and the close answered it in one paragraph.
- [ ] Three to four crossroads, each with the sides' goal cards, a guess first, the reasons of the time, and a dashed
      line.
- [ ] At least one place says where chance came in, or where another road was nearly taken.
- [ ] Mechanisms within a section got only their short version, then went straight back to the crossroads.
